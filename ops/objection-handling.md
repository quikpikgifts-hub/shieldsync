# Phase 4: Discovery | Consultation | Proposal | Close

## HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Every objection is a question in disguise. Your job is not to argue — it is to understand
what they're really asking, then answer it directly and move the conversation forward.

**Framework for every objection:**
1. Acknowledge (don't dismiss)
2. Clarify (make sure you understand the real concern)
3. Answer (directly, briefly)
4. Confirm (did that address it?) and redirect

## OBJECTION BANK

---

### "I need to think about it."

**What they're really saying:** I'm not convinced enough to decide right now.

**Don't say:** "Take all the time you need!" (kills momentum)

**Do say:**
"Totally fair — this is a real decision. Let me ask you one thing: is there a
specific part of what we talked about that you'd want to think through? I'd rather
answer it now than have it sit open."

*If they have a specific concern* → address it directly.

*If it's genuinely just timing:*
"Got it. Let me do this — I'll send you the proposal with your numbers so you have
something concrete in front of you. When I follow up on [specific day], we can go
from there. Does [day] work?"

**Never leave a 'think about it' without a specific follow-up time.**

---

### "It's too expensive / I can't afford it right now."

**What they're really saying:** I don't see enough return to justify the cost.

**Do say:**
"I hear that — and I want to make sure we're looking at this the right way.
You told me you're losing roughly [estimated monthly loss] per month in missed calls.
Our fee is [$X]/month. If we recover even a fraction of what you're currently losing,
you're ahead — usually in the first few weeks.

The question isn't whether you can afford Veridian. The question is whether you can
afford to keep losing [number] calls a month."

*If budget is a genuine hard constraint:*
"Let me look at whether there's a scaled version that makes sense for your call
volume. I don't want to lose you over pricing when the math works. Can I come back
to you tomorrow with options?"

---

### "We already have voicemail / an answering service."

**What they're really saying:** I have something in place — why do I need you?

**Do say:**
"Voicemail is passive — it waits for people to call back. Our system is active — it
reaches out to the person who just hung up, within 60 seconds, and starts a conversation.
Most people who get voicemail move on to the next option immediately. That's the gap
we close.

How many of your voicemails actually convert to callbacks? Most of our clients say
that number was a lot lower than they thought before they ran the calculator."

---

### "I don't want to feel like a robot is answering for me."

**What they're really saying:** I'm worried about brand or quality.

**Do say:**
"That concern makes total sense — and it's exactly why we configure every response
to sound like it came from your business, not a call center. We use your name, your
tone, your sign-off.

The prospect doesn't know it's automated — they know they got a response from your
business in under a minute. That's actually *better* than a delayed callback. Would
it help if I walked you through what a typical first message looks like for a business
like yours?"

---

### "What if my customers don't respond to texts?"

**What they're really saying:** I'm not sure this works for my customer type.

**Do say:**
"It depends on your industry. For most service businesses — contractors, medical,
property management, legal — response rates average around 68%. That's the benchmark
we see across industries.

The callers who would never respond to a text would probably also never leave a
voicemail and wait for a callback. You're not losing those people with us — you're
already losing them. What we capture are the people who are still open to a
conversation but need one more touch."

---

### "I don't have time to manage another system."

**What they're really saying:** I'm already overwhelmed. This sounds like more work.

**Do say:**
"I understand — and this is important. Veridian is designed to reduce your workload,
not add to it. Once we configure it, you don't touch it. You get notified when a
warm lead is ready for you — someone who already responded, said what they need, and
is expecting your call.

You're not managing a system. You're just taking calls that are already pre-qualified.
How much time per day do you currently spend chasing missed calls that went cold?"

---

### "We're too small for something like this."

**What they're really saying:** I'm not sure we have enough volume for it to make sense.

**Do say:**
"Small businesses are actually who benefit most from this. You don't have the staff
to man phones constantly — which means every missed call is a bigger percentage of
your potential growth.

If you're getting 30 calls a month and missing 8 of them, that's 8 potential customers
walking away per month. At your average customer value, that's a real number. Volume
doesn't have to be high for the math to work — it just has to matter."

---

### "How do I know this will actually work for my business?"

**What they're really saying:** I want some assurance before committing.

**Do say:**
"That's exactly the right question. We don't sell you on promises — we show you your
specific numbers before you commit. That's what the free consultation is for.

By the time you decide whether to go forward, you'll have a clear view of your miss
rate, your potential recovery number, and what we'd actually do for your business. If
the math doesn't work for you, we won't pretend it does. Fair?"

---

### "I've tried marketing services before and got burned."

**What they're really saying:** I've been disappointed before and I'm protecting myself.

**Do say:**
"I hear that. Most of what's out there sells you on traffic, leads, or impressions —
and none of it matters if the phone rings and nobody answers.

We're different in a specific way: we're not bringing you new calls. We're recovering
the calls you're already getting and losing. That's a narrower promise — and one we
can actually show you results on in the first 30 days, not 6 months from now."

---

### "Can I just try it for a month?"

**What they're really saying:** I want lower risk / I'm not sure yet.

**Do say:**
"Yes — we can do that. We start with a configuration call, get you live within a
week, and you'll have 30 days of actual data. The first monthly review is at day 30.

I want you to have results before you decide whether to continue. That's exactly
the kind of accountability we want. Let me send you what that first month looks like."

---

### "I want to talk to my partner / spouse / partner first."

**What they're really saying:** I don't have sole decision authority, or I want backup for a yes.

**Do say:**
"Absolutely — this should be a shared decision. Let me ask: when do you think you'd
have a chance to talk to them? I want to make sure I can get you the proposal before
that conversation so they have something specific to look at.

And if it would help — would they want to be on a quick call with us? I'm happy to
answer their questions directly."

## PRICE ANCHORING

Before presenting the monthly fee, anchor to the loss they've already calculated:

"So you've calculated that you're losing about [$X] per month in missed calls. Our
fee is [$Y] per month. That means if we recover [$Z in revenue] — which is
[Z / monthly loss × 100]% of what you're currently losing — you've already broken even.
Everything beyond that is pure recovery."

## CLOSING SCRIPTS

---

### The Summary Close

Use this at the end of a consultation when momentum is good:

"Let me summarize what we've covered. You're currently missing about [X] calls per
month. At [conversion rate]% and [$value] per customer, that's about [$loss] per month
you're not capturing. We'd recover roughly [68%] of those using [describe service].

The investment is [$fee]/month. The math says you break even at [breakeven amount]
in recovered revenue — and then you're ahead. Based on what you've told me, that
seems reachable in the first month or two.

Does that feel right to you? Are you ready to move forward?"

---

### The Proposal-Setting Close

Use at the end of a discovery call when they're interested but not ready to decide:

"Based on what you've told me, I'm confident there's real recovery potential here.
Here's what I'd like to do: I'll put together a proposal specific to your numbers
and send it to you by [specific day] — not a generic menu, your actual situation.

You review it, ask me any questions, and we'll schedule a follow-up on [date].
Is there anything specific you'd want to see addressed in that proposal?"

---

### The Trial Close

Use when someone is hesitant but open:

"What if we made the first 30 days entirely about proving this to you? You'll have
real results — real recovered calls, real conversations started, real bookings — by
day 30. If the math doesn't work, you stop. No long-term obligation.

Would it be fair to say you'd go forward if the first 30 days showed [specific number]
in recovered revenue?"

---

### The Urgency Close

Use for HOT leads who are still stalling:

"I want to be honest with you — every month you're not running a recovery system is
another [$loss] in missed revenue. That's not a sales tactic, that's what you told me.

I don't want to pressure you. But if you tell me 'I'm ready to move forward,' we can
have you live in 5 business days. From there, the math starts working in your favor.
What's the hesitation?"

---

### The Reverse-Risk Close

Use when risk is the primary objection:

"Let me flip this around. What's the risk if this works? You pay [$fee]/month and
capture revenue you were already losing. What's the risk if it doesn't? You spent
[$fee] and found out. The risk of not trying is the [$loss] per month you already
know is walking out the door.

Which risk is bigger?"
